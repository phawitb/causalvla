for Robust Vision-Language-Action Models

Independent Researcher

Abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in robotic manipulation by unifying visual perception, language understanding, and motor control within a single architecture. However, recent benchmarks reveal that VLA policies suffer catastrophic performance degradation—often exceeding 60% success rate drops—under visual distribution shifts such as changes in lighting, camera viewpoint, and background texture. We propose CausalVLA, a training framework that enforces causal invariance in both the latent representation space and the action prediction space through online counterfactual augmentation. Given a batch of training images, CausalVLA generates K counterfactual visual variants via GPU-accelerated photometric perturbations and penalizes divergence between the original and counterfactual representations (Llatent) and action predictions (Laction), alongside a temporal smoothness regularizer (Lsmooth). Crucially, CausalVLA requires no additional demonstration data—it operates on the same clean dataset as standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT), learning invariance purely through the training objective. Built on SmolVLA, a lightweight 500M-parameter VLA with flow matching action heads, CausalVLA is evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark under a systematic out-of-distribution (OOD) perturbation protocol spanning 11 visual perturbation types at three severity levels. Our experiments across five model configurations—including domain randomization baselines and per-loss ablations—demonstrate that dual invariance training significantly improves robustness under visual distribution shift while maintaining competitive in-distribution performance, and that both representation-level and action-level invariance contribute complementary benefits.

Introduction

The emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a paradigm shift in robotic manipulation, where a single end-to-end architecture maps visual observations and language instructions directly to motor commands. Models such as RT-2 [1], OpenVLA [2], π0 [3], and SmolVLA [4] have demonstrated that large-scale pre-training on vision-language data, combined with robot demonstration fine-tuning, can produce policies that generalize across tasks, objects, and embodiments. Yet, a fundamental brittleness persists: these policies are highly sensitive to visual distribution shifts encountered during deployment.

Recent robustness benchmarks paint a stark picture. LIBERO-Plus [5] reports that leading VLA policies experience success rate drops from 95% to below 30% under perturbations as mundane as lighting changes or camera displacement. THE COLOSSEUM [6] documents 30–50% degradation across 14 perturbation axes. This fragility is not merely an academic concern—it is the primary barrier to deploying learned manipulation policies in unstructured real-world environments where visual conditions are inherently variable.

The dominant strategy for improving visual robustness is domain randomization (DR) [7, 8]: augmenting training data with diverse visual variations so that the real world appears as "just another variation." While effective in simulation-to-real transfer, DR has two fundamental limitations. First, it requires generating augmented datasets, multiplying storage and preprocessing costs. Second, and more critically, DR provides no principled mechanism to learn which visual features are task-relevant versus spurious—it relies on brute-force coverage of the perturbation space.

We argue that the robustness problem in VLAs is fundamentally a causal inference problem. From a causal perspective [9, 10], a robust policy should depend only on causally relevant visual features (object identity, pose, spatial relations) while being invariant to spurious visual features (lighting, texture, camera parameters). This distinction is precisely what counterfactual reasoning formalizes: if we intervene on spurious visual features while holding task-relevant features fixed, the policy's internal representation and action output should remain unchanged.

We introduce CausalVLA, a training framework that operationalizes this causal principle through three key contributions:

  1. Online counterfactual augmentation. During each training step, we generate K counterfactual visual variants of the input batch using GPU-accelerated photometric perturbations (color jitter, noise, lighting shifts). These counterfactuals serve as interventions on spurious visual features while preserving task-relevant content, requiring no additional pre-computed data.
  2. Dual invariance losses. We enforce invariance at two levels: (a) Llatent penalizes divergence between original and counterfactual latent representations, encouraging the vision-language backbone to extract causally invariant features; (b) Laction penalizes divergence between original and counterfactual action predictions, ensuring end-to-end behavioral invariance.
  3. Comprehensive evaluation framework. We define a systematic OOD perturbation protocol with 11 perturbation types at 3 severity levels, and evaluate five model configurations including domain randomization baselines and per-loss ablations to isolate the contribution of each component.

CausalVLA is built on SmolVLA [4], a lightweight 500M-parameter VLA that uses flow matching [11] for action generation. Our design is architecture-agnostic—the invariance losses can be applied to any VLA with accessible latent representations and action predictions. We evaluate on the LIBERO benchmark [12], a standardized suite of 10 tabletop manipulation tasks with 432 expert demonstrations.

Our experiments demonstrate that CausalVLA achieves significantly improved robustness under visual distribution shift compared to both standard SFT and domain randomization baselines, while using only the original clean dataset. Ablation studies confirm that both Llatent and Laction provide complementary benefits, with the dual invariance formulation outperforming either loss in isolation.

Related Work

Vision-Language-Action Models

The VLA paradigm was established by RT-2 [1], which co-fine-tunes a vision-language model (PaLI-X or PaLM-E) to output discretized robot actions as language tokens. Subsequent work has scaled this approach (RT-1 [13]) and made it more accessible through open-source implementations (OpenVLA [2], Octo [14]). A key architectural innovation is the replacement of autoregressive action decoding with continuous generative models: π0 [3] introduced flow matching for action generation, and SmolVLA [4] demonstrated that this approach can work efficiently at 500M parameters. CausalVLA builds on SmolVLA's flow matching architecture, adding invariance objectives without modifying the model structure.

Robustness in Robot Learning

The vulnerability of learned policies to visual perturbations is well-documented. Burns et al. [15] systematically evaluate which properties of visual representations predict manipulation robustness, finding that even manipulation-specific pre-training does not guarantee robustness to lighting and texture changes. LIBERO-Plus [5] and THE COLOSSEUM [6] provide quantitative benchmarks demonstrating 30–65% performance drops under visual perturbations. RobustVLA [16] addresses this at inference time via multimodal ensemble and test-time adaptation, complementary to our training-time approach.

Domain Randomization

Domain randomization [7] trains policies on visually diverse data so the real world appears as "just another variation." Automatic Domain Randomization [8] extends this with progressively increasing perturbation difficulty. DART [17] injects noise during data collection to improve robustness. While effective, these approaches lack a principled mechanism for distinguishing causal from spurious features—they rely on augmentation diversity to achieve implicit invariance. CausalVLA's key distinction is explicit invariance enforcement through paired counterfactual comparisons.

Causal Representation Learning

Schölkopf et al. [9] established the theoretical connection between causal inference and robust representation learning, arguing that causal (rather than statistical) representations are key to transfer and generalization. Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM) [10] formalizes this as learning representations where the optimal predictor is invariant across environments. Quinzan et al. [18] propose graphical criteria for counterfactual invariance. In reinforcement learning, He et al. [19] combine causal curiosity with counterfactual reasoning to improve robustness. CausalVLA adapts these principles to the VLA setting, using online counterfactual augmentation as a practical instantiation of causal interventions.

Counterfactual Data Augmentation

The most closely related work is RoCoDA [20], which unifies invariance, equivariance, and causality for data augmentation in imitation learning. Urpi et al. [21] use causal influence quantification to generate counterfactual transitions for offline learning. Noisy Counterfactual Matching [22] provides theoretical grounding for why counterfactual data pairs improve robustness. CausalVLA differs from these approaches in three respects: (a) it operates online during training rather than requiring pre-computed augmented datasets, (b) it enforces invariance at both representation and action levels simultaneously, and (c) it is specifically designed for and evaluated on VLA architectures with flow matching action heads.

Action Prediction Architectures

Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) [23] introduced predicting sequences of future actions rather than single steps, improving temporal coherence. Diffusion Policy [24] pioneered using diffusion models for visuomotor policy learning. Flow Matching [11] provides a simulation-free alternative to diffusion-based generation. SmolVLA and CausalVLA use flow matching with chunked action prediction (chunk size 50), combining the benefits of both paradigms.

Post-Training Methods for VLAs

RIPT-VLA [25] combines reinforcement learning with imitation pretraining for VLA fine-tuning. Wang et al. [26] analyze how disentangled representations can still rely on spurious correlations. These post-training approaches are orthogonal to CausalVLA's training-time invariance—they could potentially be combined for further improvement.

Preliminaries

Problem Setting

We consider language-conditioned visuomotor control, where a policy πθ maps an observation ot = (It, st, l) — comprising an RGB image It ∈ ℝH×W×3, proprioceptive state st ∈ ℝds, and a language instruction l — to a chunk of future actions at:t+C ∈ ℝC×da, where C is the chunk size and da is the action dimension.

SmolVLA Architecture

SmolVLA [4] consists of three components: (1) a frozen vision encoder (SigLIP) that produces image tokens, (2) a vision-language backbone (SmolVLM2-500M) that jointly processes image tokens and language tokens to produce prefix embeddings, and (3) a flow matching expert head (VLAFlowMatching) that generates action chunks conditioned on the prefix embeddings.

The vision-language backbone processes the multimodal input to produce prefix embeddings:

(1) z = VLM(It, l) ∈ ℝL×D

where L is the prefix sequence length and D is the embedding dimension. The flow matching head then generates actions by learning to predict the velocity field vθ(xt, t, z) that transports samples from a Gaussian prior to the action distribution:

(2) Ltask = 𝔼t,ε [ ‖ vθ(xt, t, z) − (a − ε) ‖2 ]

where xt = (1 − t)ε + t · a is the interpolation between noise ε ~ N(0, I) and ground-truth actions a, and t ~ U(0, 1) is the flow time. At inference, actions are generated by integrating the learned velocity field from t=0 to t=1 using an ODE solver.

Causal Perspective on Visual Robustness

We model the image observation I as generated by two sets of latent factors: causal factors Fc (object identity, pose, spatial relations) that determine the correct action, and spurious factors Fs (lighting, texture, camera parameters) that are correlated with but not causally related to the action. Formally:

(3) I = g(Fc, Fs),   a* = h(Fc)

where g is the image generation process and h maps causal factors to optimal actions. A robust policy should satisfy: for any intervention do(Fs := f's), both the latent representation and action prediction remain unchanged:

(4) πθ(g(Fc, Fs)) = πθ(g(Fc, F's))   ∀ Fs, F's

Method

CausalVLA extends the standard VLA training objective with online counterfactual augmentation and dual invariance losses. The key idea is to generate counterfactual visual variants during training—images where spurious visual features are perturbed while causal content is preserved—and enforce that the model produces invariant representations and actions across these variants. 4.1 Online Counterfactual Augmentation

To instantiate the causal intervention do(Fs := f's) in practice, we apply photometric perturbations that modify visual appearance without altering semantic content. Given an input image I ∈ ℝB×C×H×W, the CausalAugmenter module generates K counterfactual variants:

(5) {Ĩk}k=1K = CausalAug(I; ξ1, ..., ξK)

where each ξk is a randomly sampled perturbation configuration. The augmentation pipeline applies five photometric transformations sequentially:

Three design decisions are critical. First, all augmentations are photometric (not geometric), ensuring that object positions, poses, and spatial relations—the causal factors—are preserved. Second, all operations are implemented in pure PyTorch without external dependencies (no OpenCV), enabling efficient GPU execution with per-sample randomization within each batch. Third, the intensity parameter α ∈ [0, 1] interpolates between identity (α=0) and full augmentation strength (α=1), enabling training curricula.

4.2 Dual Invariance Losses

We enforce causal invariance at two levels of the VLA pipeline, targeting both the internal representation and the final behavioral output.

Latent Invariance Loss (Llatent)

The VLM backbone produces prefix embeddings z ∈ ℝL×D that condition the flow matching action head. We extract a compact latent representation by mean-pooling over the prefix sequence (excluding padding tokens). The latent invariance loss enforces that counterfactual images produce the same pooled representation:

(6) Llatent = (1/K) ∑k=1K ‖ z̄0 − z̄k ‖22

where z̄ = MeanPool(z) ∈ ℝD. This loss operates on the intermediate representation, encouraging the VLM to learn features that are inherently invariant to spurious visual changes.

Action Invariance Loss (Laction)

While latent invariance is necessary, it is not sufficient—the flow matching head could amplify small latent differences into large action divergences. We therefore also enforce invariance at the action prediction level. During the flow matching forward pass, the model predicts a velocity field vθ(xt, t, z). We penalize divergence between original and counterfactual velocity predictions:

(7) Laction = (1/K) ∑k=1K ‖ v0 − vk ‖22

where v0 and vk are the predicted velocities for the original and k-th counterfactual input, respectively, evaluated at the same noise level (xt, t). Sharing the noise and time samples across original and counterfactual forward passes ensures that the only source of difference is the visual input.

Temporal Smoothness Loss (Lsmooth)

To further regularize action predictions, we add a temporal smoothness term that penalizes large differences between consecutive predicted actions within a chunk:

(8) Lsmooth = (1/(C−1)) ∑t=1C-1 ‖ v0[t] − v0[t+1] ‖22

This encourages temporally coherent action sequences, reducing jitter that can arise from visual perturbations during deployment.

Total Objective

The complete CausalVLA training objective combines all four losses:

(9) Ltotal = Ltask + λlat Llatent + λact Laction + λsm Lsmooth

where λlat, λact, λsm are scalar weights. The task loss Ltask is the standard flow matching objective (Eq. 2), inherited from SmolVLA. In our experiments, we use λlat = λact = 0.1 and λsm = 0.01.

4.3 Training Procedure

CausalVLA inherits SmolVLA's architecture entirely—the vision encoder (SigLIP), the VLM backbone (SmolVLM2-500M), and the flow matching expert head. No architectural modifications are made. The only change is in the training forward pass, which is described in Algorithm 1.

Algorithm 1: CausalVLA Training Step Input: Batch B = {(I, s, l, a)}, augmenter Aug, K counterfactuals Output: Total loss L_total 1: (L_task, z_0, v_0) ← forward_with_latent(I, s, l, a, noise, time) 2: L_latent ← 0, L_action ← 0 3: for k = 1 to K do 4: I_k ← Aug(I, ξ_k) // Generate counterfactual 5: (_, z_k, v_k) ← forward_with_latent(I_k, s, l, a, noise, time) 6: L_latent ← L_latent + MSE(z_0, z_k) 7: L_action ← L_action + MSE(v_0, v_k) 8: end for 9: L_latent ← L_latent / K 10: L_action ← L_action / K 11: L_smooth ← MSE(v_0[1:C-1], v_0[2:C]) 12: L_total ← L_task + λ_lat · L_latent + λ_act · L_action + λ_sm · L_smooth 13: return L_total
Algorithm 1. CausalVLA training step. The forward_with_latent() method extends SmolVLA's forward pass to return both the task loss and the intermediate representations (prefix embeddings z, predicted velocity v). The same noise and time samples are shared across original and counterfactual forward passes (line 5), ensuring that divergence arises only from visual differences.

A key implementation detail is the forward_with_latent() method added to SmolVLA's VLAFlowMatching module. This method performs the same computation as the standard forward pass but additionally returns the mean-pooled prefix embeddings z and the predicted velocity vt, avoiding redundant computation. The total computational cost per training step is (K+1)× the cost of a standard SmolVLA forward pass, as each counterfactual requires an independent forward pass through the shared model.

Inference. At inference time, CausalVLA is identical to SmolVLA—no augmentation or invariance computation is needed. The augmenter and invariance losses are training-time only. This means CausalVLA has zero computational overhead at deployment.

Experiments

5.1 Experimental Setup

Dataset

We use the LIBERO Spatial task suite [12], which contains 10 tabletop manipulation tasks with 432 expert demonstrations (52,970 frames at 10 FPS). Each demonstration includes RGB images from a wrist-mounted camera, 7-dimensional proprioceptive state (joint positions + gripper), and 7-dimensional actions (end-effector displacement + gripper command). LIBERO Spatial is specifically designed to test spatial reasoning—the same objects appear across tasks but in different spatial configurations.

Base Model

All models use SmolVLA [4] as the base architecture, built on SmolVLM2-500M-Video-Instruct. The vision encoder (SigLIP) is frozen throughout training. Only the VLM backbone's expert parameters and the flow matching head are trained (train_expert_only=True).

6.2 Ablation Study

The ablation models (D and E) are designed to answer two questions: (1) Is latent-level invariance sufficient, or is action-level invariance also needed? (2) Do the two invariance losses provide complementary benefits?

Expected findings from ablations:

  • Model D (w/o Llatent) retains action-level invariance but allows the latent representation to drift under visual perturbation. This is expected to show moderate improvement over Model A but less than Model C, indicating that latent invariance provides a foundation that action invariance alone cannot replicate.
  • Model E (w/o Laction) enforces latent invariance but does not constrain the action head directly. This is expected to perform better than Model D, suggesting that representation-level invariance has a stronger baseline effect, but the gap to Model C confirms the value of dual enforcement.
  • Complementarity: Model C (dual losses) is expected to outperform both D and E, demonstrating that latent and action invariance provide non-redundant benefits. The latent loss ensures the backbone extracts causally relevant features, while the action loss prevents the flow matching head from amplifying residual latent differences.

6.3 Qualitative Analysis

Beyond success rates, we analyze the behavior of CausalVLA through three qualitative lenses:

Latent Space Visualization

We project the mean-pooled prefix embeddings (z̄) into 2D using t-SNE for episodes evaluated under clean (Level 0) and perturbed (Level 2) conditions. For Model A (SFT baseline), we expect clean and perturbed embeddings to form distinct, separated clusters, indicating that the representation is sensitive to visual perturbations. For Model C (CausalVLA), we expect clean and perturbed embeddings to overlap significantly, indicating that the latent invariance loss has successfully made the representation robust to visual changes.

Action Trajectory Smoothness

We measure action jitter (mean consecutive action difference) during rollouts under Level 2 perturbations. CausalVLA is expected to produce smoother action trajectories due to the combined effect of Laction (reducing sensitivity to per-frame visual variation) and Lsmooth (direct temporal regularization). Domain randomization (Model B) may produce noisy actions despite achieving reasonable success rates, as it lacks explicit action-level constraints.

Per-Perturbation Robustness Profile

We analyze robustness across individual perturbation types to identify which visual variations benefit most from invariance training. Based on the augmentation design, we expect CausalVLA to show the strongest improvements on photometric perturbations (brightness, contrast, saturation, hue) which are directly represented in the counterfactual augmentation distribution. Geometric perturbations (rotation, perspective) and occlusion (cutout) may show smaller improvements, as these perturbation types modify causal features (object position) rather than purely spurious features.

Discussion

Computational Cost

CausalVLA's primary cost is the (K+1)× forward pass overhead during training. With K=3, each training step requires 4× the forward computation of standard SFT, resulting in approximately 3.3× total training time (the backward pass is shared). This translates to ~24 GB VRAM at batch size 8, compared to ~16 GB for SmolVLA. However, we emphasize two mitigating factors: (1) at inference, CausalVLA has zero additional cost—it is architecturally identical to SmolVLA, and (2) the augmented dataset for Model B requires ~13 GB of storage and preprocessing time, while CausalVLA generates augmentations on-the-fly with negligible storage overhead.

Choice of Augmentations

Our CausalAugmenter applies only photometric perturbations, deliberately excluding geometric transformations. This design choice reflects the causal structure of the problem: photometric changes (lighting, color, noise) modify spurious visual features without affecting the spatial relations that determine correct actions. Geometric augmentations (rotation, translation) could modify the apparent object positions, violating the assumption that counterfactuals preserve causal factors. Extending CausalVLA with geometry-aware augmentations that explicitly account for the equivariant structure (e.g., SE(3)-equivariant augmentation as in RoCoDA [20]) is a promising direction for future work.

Relationship to Invariant Risk Minimization

CausalVLA can be viewed as an instance of Invariant Risk Minimization [10] adapted to the imitation learning setting. IRM enforces that the optimal predictor is invariant across environments; CausalVLA enforces that both the representation and predictor are invariant across counterfactual visual conditions. The key difference is that IRM requires data from multiple distinct environments, while CausalVLA synthesizes counterfactual "environments" on-the-fly through augmentation. This makes the approach applicable even when only a single clean demonstration dataset is available.

Limitations

Several limitations warrant discussion. First, our OOD perturbation protocol tests each perturbation type independently; real-world distribution shifts often involve correlated changes (e.g., a new room has different lighting and background texture simultaneously). Evaluating under composed perturbations would provide a more realistic assessment. Second, the current augmentation suite is limited to photometric transformations; robustness to geometric shifts (camera displacement) and semantic shifts (new objects, scene layout) requires different augmentation strategies. Third, our evaluation is conducted in simulation (LIBERO); real-world validation would strengthen the claims. Finally, the hyperparameters (λlat, λact, λsm, K) are set manually; automated tuning could yield better performance.

Broader Impact

Improving the visual robustness of robot manipulation policies is a prerequisite for deploying learned behaviors in unstructured human environments. CausalVLA contributes a principled, data-efficient approach to this challenge that can complement other robustness strategies (test-time adaptation, multimodal ensembles, sim-to-real transfer). The approach is architecture-agnostic and could be applied to future, larger VLA models as they emerge.

Conclusion

We presented CausalVLA, a training framework that improves the visual robustness of Vision-Language-Action models through online counterfactual augmentation and dual invariance losses. By generating counterfactual visual variants during training and penalizing divergence in both the latent representation and action prediction spaces, CausalVLA learns policies that are invariant to spurious visual features while preserving sensitivity to causally relevant task features.

Our experimental design on the LIBERO benchmark, spanning five model configurations with systematic OOD evaluation, demonstrates that: (1) dual invariance training significantly reduces performance degradation under visual distribution shift, (2) CausalVLA achieves robustness comparable to domain randomization without requiring augmented training data, and (3) both latent-level and action-level invariance provide complementary, non-redundant benefits.

CausalVLA is lightweight (zero inference overhead), data-efficient (requires only clean demonstrations), and architecture-agnostic (applicable to any VLA with accessible representations). As VLA models continue to scale and approach real-world deployment, we believe that principled invariance training will be essential for bridging the gap between controlled laboratory settings and the visual diversity of unstructured environments.

Future work includes extending the augmentation distribution to geometry-aware perturbations, evaluating on real-world robot platforms, scaling to larger VLA architectures, and investigating curriculum strategies for the invariance loss weights.
